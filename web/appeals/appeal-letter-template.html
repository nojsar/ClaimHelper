Free appeal letter template for health insurance denials

UPDATED JULY 2026 · COPY & PASTE FREELY · NOT LEGAL OR MEDICAL ADVICE

Copy the template, replace every [bracketed] field, attach your evidence, and send it the way your denial letter instructs. The line-by-line notes below explain what makes each section persuasive.

Template — internal appeal (any denial reason)
[Your full name] [Street address, City, State ZIP] [Phone] · [Email] Member ID: [from your insurance card] · Group #: [if shown] Claim / Reference #: [from the denial letter] [Today's date] [Insurer name] Appeals Department [Appeals address — from the denial letter] RE: APPEAL of denial dated [date on the letter] — [name of the denied service, procedure, or medication] To the Appeals Department: I am formally appealing the denial of [service/medication] issued on [date]. The denial letter states the reason as: "[quote the denial reason word for word]." I ask you to reverse this decision for the following reasons: 1. MY PLAN COVERS THIS SERVICE. [Quote the section of your Evidence of Coverage or Summary Plan Description that applies — e.g., "Section 4.2 covers medically necessary diagnostic imaging."] 2. THE SERVICE IS MEDICALLY NECESSARY FOR MY CONDITION. I have been diagnosed with [diagnosis]. My treating physician, Dr. [name], has determined that [service/medication] is necessary because [one or two clinical sentences — symptoms, risks of no treatment, why alternatives are inadequate]. A letter of medical necessity is enclosed. 3. [IF APPLICABLE] ALTERNATIVES HAVE ALREADY FAILED. I have previously tried [treatments/medications with dates], which resulted in [outcome/side effects]. Records are enclosed. Enclosed with this appeal: - Letter of medical necessity from Dr. [name], dated [date] - Medical records: [list what you're attaching] - [Clinical guidelines, studies, or policy excerpts, if any] - Copy of the denial letter Please provide your written determination within the timeframes required by applicable law, and send me the complete clinical rationale and the specific policy relied upon if any part of this appeal is denied. If any information is missing from this appeal, contact me at [phone] before issuing a denial on administrative grounds. Sincerely, [Signature] [Printed name]

Line-by-line: what makes this letter work

  • Quote their denial reason verbatim. It forces the reviewer to address your rebuttal to the actual stated grounds — and prevents the goalposts from moving.
  • Quote your plan's own language. It connects your request to the coverage terms the reviewer must apply. Ask your insurer or plan administrator for the Evidence of Coverage, Summary Plan Description, and claims procedure that govern your plan.
  • Numbered arguments. Reviewers work through appeals like checklists. Make yours checkable.
  • The "contact me before denying" line may help surface missing information, but it does not require the plan to call you or extend your filing deadline.
  • The enclosures list creates a record of exactly what you submitted — useful at external review.

Urgent care? Use this opening instead

If your situation meets the plan's urgent-care standard because the ordinary appeal timeline could seriously jeopardize life, health, or the ability to regain maximum function, use an expedited opening. Many plans subject to federal claims rules use a deadline of no more than 72 hours, but plan and program rules differ — confirm the timeframe in your notice.

Expedited appeal opening
I am requesting an EXPEDITED APPEAL of the denial of [service/medication] issued on [date]. My treating physician has determined that the standard appeal timeframe would seriously jeopardize my [life / health / ability to regain maximum function] because [brief clinical reason]. Please apply the expedited timeframe stated in my plan and applicable rules. My physician's supporting statement is enclosed.

Make it stronger with a reason-specific letter

A general letter must be adapted to the reason in your denial notice. Use the matching guide to address the specific plan rule or clinical judgment in dispute:

Frequently asked questions

Does the letter need to be formal?

Organized beats eloquent. Reviewers check whether the facts and plan language support coverage, so numbered arguments with clearly labeled evidence are easier to evaluate.

How do I send it?

Exactly the way the denial letter instructs — portal, fax, or mail. Always keep proof: certified mail with return receipt, or screenshots of portal confirmations.

What if I miss something?

The "contact me before denying on administrative grounds" line helps, but double-check the denial letter's list of required elements before sending. Missing member IDs and unsigned letters cause pointless delays.
